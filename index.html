THE GUIDE

Ten years of jeep tracks,
glacier trails, and passport stamps at 4,000 metres.

This isn't a travel agency reading off a brochure. It's one traveller's logbook, turned into a way for others to follow the same routes safely.

I grew up chasing the roads most maps just draw as thin grey lines through Gilgit-Baltistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, and the hill stations of Hazara. Over the years that's turned into real routes: standing at Khunjerab Pass where Pakistan meets China, crossing Babusar Top before the snow closes it for the season, watching a polo match at 3,700 metres on Shandur Pass, and losing track of time in Hunza and Skardu more times than I can count.

What started as personal trips became something people kept asking to join. So this site exists for one reason: to take a small number of travellers Pakistani or international, along on routes I already know, on foot, by bike, or by jeep, with the logistics already worked out.

Road Trips

From easy hill-station escapes to the highest paved border crossing on earth — routes graded honestly, so you know exactly what you're signing up for.

Easy Escape

Murree & Nathia Gali

2,200–2,500m. Short drive from Islamabad, no acclimatisation needed. Good for families and first-timers.

High Altitude

Hunza, Skardu & Shandur Pass

2,200–3,700m. Full valley trips, some altitude gain, a couple of long driving days.

Extreme Altitude

Babusar Top & Khunjerab Pass

4,100–4,700m. Thin air, cold even in summer, and a seasonal window — these need proper planning.

BEFORE YOU RIDE

What to bring

The basics — I'll send a full checklist once your trip is confirmed.

01

Riding Gear

Proper riding jacket and pants, gloves, boots — layered for temperature swings from 35°C in the valleys to near-freezing at the passes.

02

Documents

CNIC or passport, bike registration/rental papers, and any NOC required for restricted sections — I confirm exactly what's needed before you travel.

03

Fitness

No technical skill required, but multiple 6–8 hour riding days at altitude are tiring — reasonable fitness helps a lot.
